## Title (their cap ~90 chars)
**Can a chip really draw power from the quantum vacuum? Let's finally test it.**

## Lay abstract (~240 chars)
A 2021 physics paper claims tiny chips harvest energy from empty space. In five years, nobody
has independently checked. I'm running the first blinded, pre-registered test — and I'll
publish the answer whether it's yes, no, or "it was the glue."

## What is the context of this research?

In 2021, a university lab published something extraordinary in a respected physics journal:
tiny electronic sandwiches (metal-insulator-metal junctions) topped with mirrored cavities
thinner than a wavelength of light appeared to produce a small electric current from nothing —
energy from the quantum vacuum itself, the researchers proposed. They patented it. A companion
paper suggested a square meter of these chips could deliver 70 watts.

Physics says the vacuum is not a battery — you can't drain empty space. So either this is one
of the most important measurements of the century, or something more ordinary is producing
that current: a chemical reaction in the palladium electrodes, a whisper of heat, a
microvolt-scale electrical artifact. Here's the strange part: **in five years, no independent
lab has checked.** No replication, no published raw data, no experimental critique. Earlier
this year, a startup raised $12 million to commercialize the idea — still with zero
independent tests on record.

## What is the significance of this project?

Science works because extraordinary claims get tested by people with nothing riding on the
answer. When that step gets skipped, claims don't get resolved — they linger, gather patents,
and attract investment on untested physics. This project closes that gap for one specific,
concrete, five-year-old claim.

Every outcome is worth knowing and will be published: if the effect is real, the world should
urgently know. If it's an artifact — my honest expectation, and I state my odds publicly:
about 3% it's real, 55% it's a nameable artifact, 42% it simply doesn't reproduce — then a
patented, venture-funded, peer-reviewed claim gets the correction the scientific record needs.
Every protocol, analysis rule, and expectation is already frozen and public at
github.com/meanmod3/zpe-lab, locked in BEFORE any device exists so I can't move the goalposts
afterward — you can audit exactly what I'll do with the data before you back this.

## What are the goals of the project?

This campaign funds **the device fabrication run** — the first physical step: a professional
nanofabrication facility (quotes in progress at RIT and Texas A&M — deliberately not the
original researchers' university) manufactures the chips to the published recipe, plus the
control the original work never included: identical chips with **palladium, platinum, and
nickel electrodes side by side**. Palladium-on-alumina is literally how hydrogen sensors are
built; if the "vacuum" signal follows the metal's hydrogen chemistry instead of the cavity,
that single comparison resolves the mystery. The chips then enter a blinded test protocol —
labels hidden from the analyst, decoy runs to catch equipment artifacts, and a calibration
exercise on known physics that my setup must pass before it's allowed to measure anything
contested. Instruments and the measurement campaign are funded separately (grant applications
are pending and disclosed); if this campaign succeeds, the chips exist no matter what.
