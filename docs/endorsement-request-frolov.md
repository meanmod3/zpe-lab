Dear Prof. Frolov,

I'm writing because you are one of the few physicists who has made a public practice of
holding bold, under-replicated experimental claims to account, and I would value exactly that
scrutiny on a project — including a "this isn't worth your time" verdict, which is a useful
answer too.

I'm an independent researcher preparing the first independent replication of Moddel et al.,
"Casimir-cavity-induced conductance changes" (Phys. Rev. Research 3, L022007, 2021) and the
companion Symmetry paper — the claim that MIM tunnel junctions with optical cavities draw
zero-bias current from vacuum fluctuations. I verified the state of the record before
committing to this: five years on, there is no independent replication, no public raw data,
and no experimental critique, while a startup commercializing the device class raised $12M
this May. I am not a physicist by training, which is precisely why I've built the project to
be checkable rather than trusted.

The whole methodology is pre-registered and public before any device exists
(github.com/meanmod3/zpe-lab): blinded run labels, cryptographically sealed instrument
constants, interleaved decoy runs that separate drive-correlated artifacts from
cavity-correlated signal, a quantified seven-class artifact budget with a frozen 3-sigma
criterion, and a full adversarial-review history — including a round that blocked my own
analysis pipeline after demonstrating it could be fooled, with running code. It also adds a
control the original work never ran: matched Pd/Pt/Ni electrodes in one fabrication run, to
test the palladium-hydride pathway (Pd/Al2O3 being the standard hydrogen-sensor stack). My
pre-registered expectation is that the effect resolves to a mundane artifact or a null.

Two specific things I'd genuinely value, either or both, at whatever depth your time allows:

1. **A skeptical read of the pre-registration** before hardware exists — the artifact budget
   and the blinding/decoy design are exactly where an experienced condensed-matter eye would
   catch what I've missed. Finding a hole now is worth far more than finding it after the run.
2. **If (and only if) you judge the design sound**, a willingness to be named as an advisory
   reviewer would materially help the project's credibility with facilities and funders — but
   I'm asking for your assessment first, and an endorsement second and only if earned.

I'm not asking you to vouch for a result — there is no result yet, and I'd be wary of anyone
who offered to. I'm asking whether the design is good enough to produce a trustworthy one. If
your answer is "no" or "not interested," that's genuinely fine and I'll have learned something
either way.

Thank you for considering it.
