**Substrate:** I'll provide the wafers — Si with 300 nm thermal SiO2 — so there's no oxide

**Wafer size:** whatever your tools run most cleanly — I'd assume 100 mm (4"). The devices are

**On the Al2O3 barrier:** understood that ~1.3 nm is near the edge of controllable at ~1

better-controlled barrier that I can characterize honestly is more valuable to me than a
nominal 1.3 nm I can't trust, so I'd welcome your process owner's judgment on the tightest
thickness he'd be confident specifying and measuring.

**On the electrode materials — this is my main open question.** The comparison at the heart
of the experiment is a *palladium* top electrode against platinum and nickel controls,
fabricated in the same run. I understand you stock only Ni sputter targets, with Pt available
as e-beam pellets for limited staff runs. Two questions:
1. Could I **supply Pd** (a sputter target, or e-beam pellets) for a staff run on your tools —
   and if so, in what form and purity would you need it?
2. If customer-supplied Pd isn't workable, is Pd available at all via e-beam evaporation on
   your end? Splitting Pd to a different facility would break the "all electrodes in one run"
   control, so keeping Pd/Pt/Ni together matters a lot to the design.
